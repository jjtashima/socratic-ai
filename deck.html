What this actually is

And why it has never worked at scale.

Most of you already do this.

  • Office hours. One student, one question at a time.
  • A lab teacher working beside someone until they see it.
  • An ESL instructor drawing a sentence out instead of supplying it.
  • A trainer asking what would happen if you did it the other way.

You just might not have called it that.

The definition

Socratic questioning isn't asking a lot of questions.

It's asking questions whose answers you don't supply.

Restraint, not interrogation. In a good seminar, the person leading it is working hard to not say the thing.

Three things made that work.

That's the whole list.

I've been at this a year and found a lot more than three. These are the three that matter on day one. The rest is on the handout.

One

Say what the conversation should produce.

My AI coach requested me to write down my goal and my system in one paragraph. My writing was a little messy before because I wrote everything down separately. My paragraph looked better and made more sense when I changed it.
— HSENG065 student reflection

It ends when a thing exists — not when the student is tired.

Two

Tell it what to do with each kind of answer.

Answer like this →
ask this
Answer like that →
ask something different
Answer like the third →
ask a third thing

A prompt that reacts is a chatbot. A prompt that sorts is a teacher. This is what you'll build after the break.

Three

Take a good answer when you get one.

I did realize though that the only sentence that needed to be revised was that one, which made me feel incredible, I honestly thought that I would need help in various areas of my paragraph.
— HSENG065 student reflection
The AI didn't overwhelm me it focused on one main thing at a time, which made the revision feel easier and less stressful.
— HSENG065 student reflection
Why that third one matters most

A coach built to hold back does something no worksheet can.

The students furthest from academic English get to keep their own words.

I looked at who my coach was rewriting. It was rewriting the students whose English was least standard, and leaving the polished ones alone. It wasn't judging ideas — it was judging how the sentence sounded.

And the honest one
Sometimes it asks a lot of questions and I think I'm done and then it wants more information.
— HSENG065 student reflection

That's a real limitation, and a student found it before I did. When these go wrong, this is usually how — not by being too easy. By being too relentless.

The one thing I wish someone had told me

Eventually a student will tell it something serious.

  • If it's about right now — stop coaching. Stay warm, don't probe, name a person.
